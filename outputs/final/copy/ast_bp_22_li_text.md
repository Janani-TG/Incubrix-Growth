## 1. Publication-Ready Post Copy

**Post Hook:** The Mathematics of SaaS Valuation: Why a 5% Retention Increase Compounds Enterprise Value.

**Post Body:**
In early-stage B2B software, founders frequently over-index on top-of-funnel gross new additions while under-investing in cohort stabilization.

According to classical unit economics research (Bain & Company / HBR), compounding customer retention generates substantially higher valuation multiples than high-churn top-line expansion.

Here is the mathematical comparison:

Company A (High Churn / Leaky Bucket):
- Acquires 1,000 customers annually
- Annual net logo retention: 65%
- Outcome: At Year 3, the customer base plateaus at ~2,100 accounts despite acquiring 3,000 total customers. Ad spend hits a ceiling.

Company B (Compounding Retention / Growth Engine):
- Acquires the same 1,000 customers annually
- Annual net logo retention: 85%
- Outcome: At Year 3, the customer base expands to ~2,800 active accounts, with expansion revenue compounding on stabilized cohorts.

The Takeaway for Technical Founders:
Retention is not a customer success problem—it is an engineering telemetry problem. When you instrument Time-to-Value, remove early onboarding friction, and automate user activation loops, compounding economics follow.
